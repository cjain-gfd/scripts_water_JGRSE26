# Captions for animations
Captions for animations S1-S9 are provided here. As the visualisation criteria for the cell-based composition field is the same for all animations, the detailed explanation is only provided in the caption of animation S1.

### Animation S1
Temporal evolution of the full annulus model *AT19r1* with unlimited water input, adaptive friction coefficient and 1900 K initial mantle potential temperature. The four annuli represent different fields: composition, water, temperature (with isotherms of 1000, 2000, 3000 K as white lines) and viscosity. The cell-based composition field represents the following different material: TTG (>= 60% in tangerine), basalt (>= 60% in dark purple), harzburgite (>= 40% in teal, lighter shades represent higher harzburgite content and mantle depletion, TTG-melt (>= 50% in peach-orange), basaltic-melt (>= 50% in sky blue), harzburgitic-melt (>= 50% in white), and TTG-basalt-mix (>= 40% TTG and >= 40% basalt in light purple). The empty cells in black represent mixes of different materials that do not fit either of the above criteria.

### Animation S2
Temporal evolution of the quadrant annulus model *QW5r1* with 5 ocean masses (OM) of water, flood basalt eruption, adaptive friction coefficient and 1900 K initial mantle potential temperature. The four quadrants represent different fields, counter-clockwise from top left: composition, water, viscosity, temperature with isotherms of 1000, 2000, 3000 K (white lines).

### Animation S3
Temporal evolution of the quadrant annulus model *QW10hr1* with 10 OM of water, flood basalt eruption, adaptive friction coefficient and 1900 K initial mantle potential temperature. In this model, the minimum concentration of water required in a cell to generate felsic melts from wet basalts is prescribed as 0.5 wt.% (5000 ppm). The four quadrants represent different fields, counter-clockwise from top left: composition, water, viscosity, temperature with isotherms of 1000, 2000, 3000 K (white lines).
